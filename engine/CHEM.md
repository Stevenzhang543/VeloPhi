# VeloPhi chemistry core — data and operations (26.64, D261)

`engine/velophi-chem` is the chemistry core: species and reactions from sourced data, aqueous equilibrium, kinetics,
heat, phases, gases, electrochemistry, the bench session, molecules and crystals, nuclides and the reactor workshop.
It follows the rules of the physics core (`PORTING.md`): pure Rust, no clocks or threads, only IEEE-754 basic
operations (`+ − × ÷ √`) and the deterministic elementary functions of `velophi_core::dmath`; iterated collections
are sorted vectors or `BTreeMap`s; iteration counts depend on the data only. The WebAssembly build, the native build
and the Node-API test module therefore give bit-identical results (`tests/chem/determinism.test.ts`).

There is no TypeScript reference for chemistry. Its independent references are fixed data computed by open-source
Python packages and closed-form solutions (`scripts/oracles/chem_*.py`, `nuclear.py`, `reactor.py` →
`tests/fixtures/chem/*.json`), compared with stated tolerances (`docs/chem/VALIDATION.md`).

## Interface (ABI 3)

- Stateless operations go through `calc_call({ op, … })` exactly like the lab operations: the ABI layer tries
  `velophi_chem::api::calc` first and falls through to the physics core. Front end: `calc()` of `src/engine/core.ts`,
  typed wrappers in `src/engine/chem.ts`, `src/engine/nuclear.ts`, `src/engine/mol.ts`.
- The bench is a session of its own kind: `chem_session_create({ bench? }) → handle`,
  `chem_session_call(h, { op, … }) → container`, `chem_session_output(h)`, `chem_session_free(h)`
  (`CoreBackend.chemSessionCreate / chemSessionCall / chemSessionFree`).
- Results are VRD1 containers (`ABI.md`); texts are messages `{ key, params }`; a failed request returns 0 and the
  message at `last_error`.
- Data are not compiled into the core. The front end loads the JSON data files on first use and hands them over with
  a `…SetData` operation (they are global to the core instance, like `setLibrary` and `setModels`).
- `tests/engine/ops.test.ts` checks that every operation the front end sends is implemented and that every
  implemented operation is listed in the tables below (first column, in backticks). A request names its operation
  with the property `op`; the entries of a bench log name theirs with `act`, so the two never mix.

Diagnostics have one shape everywhere:
`{ code: string, severity: 'info' | 'approx' | 'unmodelled' | 'error', path?: string, params?: object }`.
The interface renders the text; `unmodelled` is the source of the “未建模” mark (D273).

## Data files

All data are JSON under `src/chem/data/`, generated by scripts under `scripts/chem/` (reproducible: same inputs, same
bytes) and loaded on demand by the front end. Every record names its source (`src`); values typed from a reference
by hand are marked as such and listed in `docs/chem/DATA_SOURCES.md`.

### The aqueous network — `aqueous.json` (`velophi-chemdb` 1)

Built by `scripts/chem/build_aqueous.py` from the PHREEQC database files of the U.S. Geological Survey
(`phreeqc.dat`, `wateq4f.dat`, `minteq.v4.dat`) and the hand-entered `scripts/chem/supplement*.dat` (PHREEQC syntax).
The same network is written as a PHREEQC database, `tests/fixtures/chem/velophi.dat`, so the reference program
computes with exactly the constants and the activity model the core uses.

- `components`: ids; the first two are `H2O` and `H+`. Oxidation states are components of their own (`Fe+2` and
  `Fe+3`; `NO3-`, `NO2-` and `NH4+`; `SO4-2`, `SO3-2`, `S2O3-2` and `HS-`; `Cl-` and `ClO-`; `O2`, `H2`, `H2O2`,
  `I2`, `Br2` …): electron transfer is a declared reaction with a rate, never an instant equilibrium (D264, D265).
  Groups that do not come apart at the bench are components too (`Acetate-`, `Oxalate-2`, `Edta-4`,
  `Thiocyanate-` …).
- `species[]`: `id`, `pq` (its name in `velophi.dat`), `phase` (`aq`, `s`, `g`), `z`, `comp` (elements), `M`
  (g/mol), `component?`, `rxn` (formation from components `{ id: ν }`), `logK` (six analytic coefficients of
  log10 K(T) = A1 + A2·T + A3/T + A4·log10 T + A5/T² + A6·T²), `gamma?` (`[a, b]` of the WATEQ Debye–Hückel form),
  `src`. A solid named like a dissolved species carries the suffix `(s)` (`Cu(OH)2(s)`); most solids keep the
  mineral names of the sources (`Calcite`, `Cerargyrite` = AgCl, `Barite`, `Halite` …) — the catalogue gives every
  species its formula and names for display.
- `couples[]`: the half-reactions the sources define (`id`, `rxn` with `e-`, `logK`), for electrode potentials.
- `dh`: Debye–Hückel A(T) and B(T), 0 … 100 °C.

Activity coefficients exactly as PHREEQC assigns them: `gamma` given → log γ = −A z² √I / (1 + a B √I) + b I;
none and neutral → 0.1·I; none and charged → Davies. One deliberate difference: the Davies equation is evaluated
at I = 1 mol/kg above that ionic strength (flag `equil.beyondActivityModel`). Activity of water: 1 − 0.017·Σm.

### The catalogue — `catalog.json` (`velophi-chemcat` 1)

Built by `scripts/chem/build_catalog.py` from the source tables under `scripts/chem/catalog/`. Sections:

- `substances[]` — species outside the network: metals and other solids that do not dissolve by themselves, table
  salts, liquids, gases, inert solutes. `id` (`Zn(s)`, `MnO2(s)`, `KNO3(s)`, `C2H5OH(l)`, `C2H4(g)`,
  `Glucose(aq)`), `formula`, `comp`, `phase` (`s`, `l`, `g`, `aq`), `M`; thermodynamics `Hf` (kJ/mol, 298.15 K),
  `S` and `cp` (J/(mol·K)); `rho` (g/cm³); `Tm`, `Tb` (K), `Hfus`, `Hvap` (kJ/mol); `antoine` `[A, B, C, Tmin, Tmax]`
  (log10 p/bar = A − B/(T/K + C)) for volatile liquids; `Tign` (K) when it burns in air;
  `dissolves` `{ species id: ν }` — what one mole becomes in water (network species or `aq` substances; `H2O` for
  water of crystallisation); `solubility` `{ points: [[T/K, g of the anhydrous salt per 100 g water], …], src }` for
  a *table salt*: it dissolves up to the handbook solubility and crystallises above it (a concentration product from
  the table, flagged approximate — the textbook solubility curve, not an activity model); `Hsol` (kJ/mol, enthalpy of
  solution) when not given by the network; `extracts` `{ solute id: partition coefficient }` for an immiscible
  solvent; `area` — see forms.
  Clarifications of the catalogue's source tables (`catalog/substances.json`): a record with `overlay: true` and the
  `id` of a network solid (`Halite`, `Chalcanthite`, `Portlandite` …) defines no species — it adds `rho`, `Tm`,
  `dissolves` and the `solubility` table to that solid (its `dissolves` is in ions, `Ca+2` + 2 `OH-`, so that the
  water of crystallisation can be told from the anhydrous salt). ν of `H2O` in `dissolves` is negative when
  dissolving consumes water (`Na2O(s)`, `P4O10(s)`). `solubility` also has `entry` (`hand`) and, where the curve is
  not rising, `shape` (`max`: sodium sulfate, sodium carbonate, the vitriols; `decreasing`: calcium hydroxide); the
  table is on the anhydrous basis, so a hydrate and its anhydrous salt share one. `crystallises` (optional id) names
  the solid that comes out of solution instead of the record's own (anhydrous `CuSO4(s)` → `Chalcanthite`; not yet
  read by the core). Provenance: `src` and `entry` describe `Hf`, `S`, `cp` (`NASA Glenn via Cantera` / `file` with
  the NASA species in `nasa`; `CODATA`, `NBS`, `CRC` / `hand`; both null when no value is known), `propSrc` the
  physical constants; the keys are spelled out in the catalogue's `sources`. A value that is not known is `null`.
  No record uses `area`.
- `thermo.Hf` — ΔfH° (kJ/mol) of the network's components; with the reaction enthalpies of the network this fixes
  the enthalpy of every network species. It may also name other network species whose tabulated value takes
  precedence over the derived one (the gases, `OH-`, `HCO3-`, `Cl2` …); `thermo.src` and `thermo.entry` (`hand`,
  `derived`) give the source per id, `null` means unknown.
- `forms` — `{ form id: specific area in m² per cm³ of solid }` for `powder`, `granule`, `chip`, `ribbon`, `foil`,
  `lump`, `wire` (the catalogue adds `crystal`, `pellet`, `sheet`, `rod`), and `precipitates[]`: the network solids
  that form from solution at the bench (fresh precipitates, and the salts that crystallise on evaporation or
  cooling; every other network solid only dissolves). Every network solid is classified with its reason in
  `scripts/chem/catalog/solids_review.tsv`.
- `display` — `{ species id: { f: "CaCO₃", name: { zh, en, de }, color?: [r, g, b] (0 … 255, solids and liquids),
  texture?: "powder" | "crystal" | "floc" | "gel" | "metal" | "liquid" } }` for network species and substances.
- `spectra` — `{ species id: [ε at 400, 420, …, 700 nm] }` in L/(mol·cm), 16 values; `spectraSrc` per species.
- `flames` — `{ element: [[λ/nm, relative intensity], …] }` for flame tests.
- `reagents[]` — what stands on the shelf: `id`, `name { zh, en, de }`, `group`, `kind` (`solution`, `solid`,
  `liquid`, `gas`), `label` (e.g. `1 mol/L`), `recipe`: for a solution `{ water_kg: 1, species: { id: mol } }` and
  `rho` (g/mL at 20 °C); for a pure substance `{ species: { id: 1 } }` and `form`; `hazard[]` (keys).
  In the catalogue: `group` is one of `acid`, `base`, `saltSolution`, `solidSalt`, `metal`, `oxide`, `nonmetal`,
  `indicator`, `organic`, `gas`, `solvent`; `hazard` keys are `corrosive`, `toxic`, `flammable`, `oxidiser`,
  `irritant`, `environment`; `label` is language-neutral (a concentration, or the formula of a pure reagent), and
  name plus label is unique per language. A mixture (crude salt, soda lime, brass, air) is a recipe of several
  species in moles, taken by mass like a pure solid. `rho` is `null` when no density is known, and `rhoEst: true`
  marks a density estimated as 0.9982 + c·M/1000 g/mL (a labelled molarity is exact either way: the recipe is
  computed from it with that density). `beyondModel: true`: concentrated sulfuric acid. Reactions, dissolution
  rates, spectra and indicator solutions whose species the network does not carry are left out of the catalogue and
  listed in `pending[]` (`section`, `id`, `missing[]`); couples of the electrochemical series with such species stay
  in `couples[]` and are listed there too.
- `reactions[]` — the declared reactions (kinetics, D265; source table `catalog/reactions.json`, checked by
  `scripts/chem/check_reactions.py`): `id`, `name { zh, en, de }`, `eq` `{ species id: ν }` (negative: consumed).
  `eq` names any species of the network — written as the textbook writes the equation (`OH-`, `Cl2`, `Cr2O7-2` …), the
  core works on components — or a substance; a gas formed in solution by its dissolved id (`H2`, `Cl2`), a gas taken
  from or released into the gas space by its `(g)` id. A rate is the rate of advancement of `eq` as written; entries
  may share one `eq` (parallel paths add up). `rate`: `{ law: "instant", lnK?, reversible? }` (to the limiting
  reagent; with `lnK: [a, b]`, ln K = a + b/T, to the extent at which the reaction quotient reaches K, and with
  `reversible: true` backwards as well), `{ law: "mass", k, orders: { id: n }, Ea, Tref }` (mol/(kg water·s);
  activities: a dissolved species m·γ, a solid or liquid 1 when present, a gas its partial pressure in atm; `orders`
  may name species outside `eq`, such as `OH-`), `{ law: "surface", k, orders, Ea, Tref, on? }` (mol/(m²·s) on the
  solid `on`, else on the first consumed solid), `{ law: "thermal", A, Ea, of? }` (first order in the amount of
  `of`, else of the first consumed species, s⁻¹), `{ law: "catalytic", catalyst, k, orders, Ea, Tref }` (mol/s per
  mole of catalyst present: k·Π aⁿ·n(catalyst)), `{ law: "none" }` (declared not to happen — passivation, an inert
  pair) and `{ law: "unmodelled" }` (it happens in reality, but its products are outside the model — zinc with
  nitric acid). An entry of these two laws names the species it is about with `between: [ids]` (it then needs no
  `eq`); when all of them are in a vessel and `needs` holds, the bench reports the diagnostic `reaction.none`
  (info) or `reaction.unmodelled` (unmodelled, the mark of D273) with the entry's `id`, and the interface shows its
  `note`. `Ea` in kJ/mol; `Tref` 298.15 K when absent. `needs` `{ Tmin?, Tmax?, ignite?, light?, min?: { id:
  activity as above — 1 for a solid or liquid that must be present }, max?: { id: activity } (no hydrogen from a
  metal while nitrate is there, the upper flammability limit), unsaturated?: [network solids] (runs only while
  these are undersaturated: lead stops in hydrochloric or sulfuric acid once its salt coats it) }`; a pH window is
  a `min` on `H+` and one on `OH-`. `effects[]` (keys: `bubbles`, `flame`, `smoke`,
  `fog`, `glow`, `spark`, `light`, `pop`, `explosion`, `heat`, `melt`, `dart`, `hiss`, `crackle`, `deposit`, `mirror`,
  `char`, `molten`) and `flameColour?` (`pale`, `paleBlue`, `blue`, `yellow`, `white`, `lilac`);
  `class` (`redox`, `displacement`, `decomposition`, `combustion`, `organic`, `synthesis`), `src`, and
  `teaching: true` when the rate constant or a threshold of `needs` is an order-of-magnitude classroom value rather
  than a measured one (`basis` then names the observation it rests on; a measured value has `entry: "hand"` and its
  citation in `src`). `note { zh, en, de }` is what the interface tells the user (limits of the model, safety of the
  real experiment); `curated` is `org01` … `org20` (the twenty organic reactions of D272, titled in the section
  `curated`) or `test` (a functional-group test); `via: [oxidant couple, reductant couple]` names the couples whose
  E° give `lnK`. Carried in the data but not used by the core's reader (`catalog.rs`): `rate.gas` (the measured
  gas-phase rate law of a reaction treated as instant), `lnK` with a `mass` law (the equilibrium a reversible slow
  reaction ends at: net rate = forward rate·(1 − Q/K)), `beyondModel: true` on a reaction (a reagent outside the
  activity model takes part), `flameColour`, `basis`, `via`, `curated`, `entry`.
- `dissolution` — `{ network solid: { kAcid, nAcid, EaAcid, kNeutral, EaNeutral, kCarbonate?, nCarbonate?,
  EaCarbonate? } }`: mol/(m²·s) = (kAcid·a(H⁺)^nAcid + kNeutral + kCarbonate·p(CO₂)^nCarbonate)·(1 − Ω) for a solid
  in a coarse form; each k at 298.15 K with its Ea in kJ/mol; p(CO₂) is the pressure of carbon dioxide (atm) the
  solution is in equilibrium with (the constants of the carbonates are converted to this convention in the source
  table, see its `dissolutionNote`).
- `electrolysis.overpotential` — `{ gas: { electrode: V } }` for the gases `H2`, `O2`, `Cl2` and the element
  symbol of the electrode's substance (`Pt`, `C`, `Cu` …): the stated overpotential of gas evolution (D266); where
  the table has no entry the core falls back on its own defaults. The other keys of `electrolysis` (`electrodes`,
  `activeAnode`, `cathode`, `chloride`) describe the classroom rules and are not read.
- `couples[]` — electrode couples ox + n e⁻ → red (source table `catalog/couples.json`): `id` (`Zn+2/Zn`), `ox`
  `{ id: ν }`, `red` `{ id: ν }` (with `H+`, `OH-`, `H2O` where the half-reaction has them; gases by their `(g)`
  id), `n`, `E0` (V at 25 °C), `inert: true` when the couple is thermodynamically possible but too slow to count at
  an electrode (nitrate, sulfate, perchlorate: never chosen in the discharge order). Not read by the core:
  `medium?` (`acid`, `base`), `src`, `entry` (`hand`, `network` — computed from the half-reactions of
  `aqueous.json` —, `computed`, `exact`), `bench: false` when a participant never stands on the bench (shown in
  the electrochemical series only), `note?`. The bench warns (`unmodelled`) when a strong oxidant and a strong
  reductant coexist and no declared reaction covers the pair, instead of inventing a reaction.

## Gas equilibrium operations (`velophi-chem/src/gibbs/api.rs`)

High-temperature gas phase (D264): an ideal mixture of the species of `gas.json` (`velophi-chemgas` 1, built by
`scripts/chem/build_gas.py`: NASA 7-coefficient polynomials of NASA TM-4513 as distributed with Cantera, standard
pressure 1 bar; ids are those of the bench, `CO2(g)`). The composition minimises the Gibbs energy (fixed pressure)
or the Helmholtz energy (fixed volume) under the element balances — the iteration of Gordon & McBride (NASA
RP-1311) with their step control; an adiabatic state is found by bisection on the temperature between 200 and
6000 K, every step a full equilibrium. Species without one of the elements present are left out. Mole fractions
below 10⁻¹² are not determined reliably and not reported. Not modelled: condensed phases (liquid water, soot),
real-gas effects, ions. Energies in J, entropies in J/K, per mole where a species is meant.

| op | Parameters | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `gasSetData` | `gas` (the `velophi-chemgas` document) | `species` (count), `elements[]` |
| `gasSpecies` | — | `species[]`: `id, comp, M, Tmin, Tmax`; `elements[]`, `P0` (Pa) |
| `gasThermo` | `ids[]`, `T?` (K, 298.15) | `T, P0`, `values[]`: `id, cp, h, s, g, extrapolated` (standard state; `h` includes the enthalpy of formation) |
| `gasReaction` | `species { id: ν }` (products positive), `T?` | `T, dH, dS, dG, lnK, log10K` (pressures in units of `P0`), `dn` (Σν), `balanced`, `extrapolated` |
| `gasEquilibrium` | `amounts { id: mol }`, `T?` (K; the temperature of the reactants when `adiabatic`), `pressure_atm?` (1) or `volume_L?`, `adiabatic?` (false: at `T`; true: the enthalpy — at fixed volume the internal energy — of the reactants is kept), `species?[]` (candidates besides what was put in; default: every species of the data), `minFraction?` | `ok`; `T, P_atm, V_L, n`, `species[]`: `id, n, x` (largest first); `H, U, S, G, cp` (frozen composition), `mass_g, density` (kg/m³), `iterations`, `adiabatic`, `initial { T, P_atm, V_L, H, U }` (the unreacted mixture), `diagnostics[]`: `gas.extrapolated` (`T, species[]`), `gas.wouldCondense` (`species, p, saturation` in atm: water vapour above its saturation pressure — the model has no liquid), `gas.highPressure` (above 10 atm: ideal gas is approximate). Not solved: `ok: false` with `gas.noSpecies`, `gas.notConverged` or `gas.adiabaticOutOfRange` (`above, Tmin, Tmax`) — never unconverged numbers |

## Colour operations (`velophi-chem/src/color/api.rs`)

What a mixture looks like (D267). A spectrum is sixteen molar absorption coefficients at 400, 420, … 700 nm
(`catalog.spectra`, L/(mol·cm), display approximations built by `scripts/chem/build_spectra.py`). The absorbance of a
layer is Σ ε·c·l (Beer–Lambert); the light of a D65 source that passes is weighted with the CIE 1931 2° colour matching
functions (`color/tables.rs`, generated) and converted to sRGB, each tristimulus value relative to the same sum without
absorption, so clear water is exactly white. A species without a spectrum is colourless. Flames: the lines of
`catalog.flames` weighted with the same functions, shown at full brightness; a colour outside the gamut of the screen
is moved towards white until it fits.

| op | Parameters | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `chemColour` | `species { id: mol/L }`, `path_cm?` (1) | `rgb [r, g, b]` (0 … 255), `absorbance[16]` of the layer, `wavelengths[16]` (nm), `colourless[]` (the ids given that have no spectrum) |
| `chemSpectrum` | `id` | `id, wavelengths[16], epsilon[16]`; `epsilon: null` for a species without a spectrum |
| `chemFlame` | `elements[]` (symbols) | `rgb` (null when none of them colours a flame), `lines[]`: `element, nm, power, rgb`; `none[]` (elements without lines) |

A vessel in `state` and `read`: `id, kind, T (K), volume_mL, capacity_mL, mass_g, closed, P (Pa), heater (W), bath (K or
null), ignited, lit, cracked, spilled_mL, outlet (id or null), collect, gas_mL (the gas held, at the room's pressure),
solids[] { id, form, n, g }, liquids[], gas[], released[], solutes[] { id, n }`, and with a solution `pH, I, water_g`.
`cracked` (26.65): the vessel broke (see the events below) and takes nothing since; `spilled_mL`: the liquid that ran
out of it onto the bench so far (it left the bench's chemistry). How it looks (see
"Colour operations"): `width_cm` (the light path: the inner width of a cylinder one and a half times as tall as wide
that holds the capacity), and with a liquid `colour [r, g, b]` (white light through that width) and `absorbance[16]`
(per cm, at 400 … 700 nm: what a spectrophotometer reads); with gas in the head space `gasColour [r, g, b]`.

A circuit in `state`: `id, a, b` (each `vessel, material, massChange_g, deposit[]`), `bridge, load, source, on,
connected, emf` (V: the electromotive force of a cell, or the decomposition voltage under a source), `I` (A, positive
when `a` is the anode), `U` (V across the load), `P` (W), `internal` (Ω), `charge` (C passed), `anode` (`a` or `b`),
`anodeCouple`, `cathodeCouple` (ids of `catalog.couples`). Electrode potentials are Nernst's with the activities of the
vessel; a product that is not there yet counts as a gas at 1 atm, a pure solid, or a dissolved species at 10⁻⁶; gas
evolution carries a tabulated overpotential (stated approximations in `echem.rs`); the cathode runs the couple with
the highest effective potential, the anode the lowest (an electrode dissolves when that is the easiest oxidation);
couples marked `inert` in the catalogue never run. The internal resistance is an estimate from the ionic strength.

`custom` of the document — what the file defines for itself (D263): `acids[]` `{ base, z?, M?, pKa[], forms?[] }` (the
fully deprotonated form `base` becomes a component of this bench; `pKa` in the order of dissociation, strongest
first; `forms` names H₁B, H₂B …) and, in the catalogue's format, `substances`, `reagents`, `reactions`, `couples`,
`dissolution`. They extend a copy of the database that belongs to this bench alone; their source reads `user`, and
`state.custom[]` lists what could not be resolved. Their enthalpies are unknown unless given: the bench then reports
`thermal.unknownEnthalpy`.

Objects of the document: `{ id, kind, capacity_mL, heat_J_K?, loss_W_K?, closed?, collect?, brim_mL?, burst_kPa?,
heatable?, shock_K?, … }` — the core reads these fields, keeps every other field as given (positions, labels) and creates
one vessel per object. The four limits of 26.65 (D297 item 9) are given by the interface from `apparatus.json` and decide
what follows by itself (the events `overflow`, `stopper.blown`, `vessel.cracked` below); an object without them behaves as
in 26.64 (more liquid than the capacity is refused with `chem.vesselFull`, a stopper holds any pressure, nothing cracks):

- `brim_mL` — the volume to the brim: an open vessel holds up to it, liquid above it runs over (a closed vessel still
  refuses what does not fit);
- `burst_kPa` — the overpressure its stopper holds; the interface gives 50 kPa (a rubber stopper pushed in by hand, a
  classroom figure) to the vessels the user puts on the bench — a guided experiment's vessels are sealed as its set-up
  needs and carry none;
- `heatable` (default true) — false for glassware that must not be heated directly (measuring cylinder, volumetric
  flask, gas jar …);
- `shock_K` — the temperature difference the glass stands when something colder is poured in hot (100 K glass, 150 K
  porcelain: classroom figures, not measured).

`fixtures[]` of the document (26.65, D297) — the things on the bench that are not vessels: stands with a clamp and a ring,
tripods with gauze, test-tube racks, burners, hot plates, baths, pneumatic troughs, reagent bottles of the shelf,
delivery tubes. Each is `{ id, kind, x, y, … }` with the fields of its kind (`src/chem/rig.ts`). They are drawing only:
the core keeps the list as given, writes it back in `document`, replays it unchanged, leaves it out of the state, the
fingerprint and undo. Where geometry decides chemistry the interface turns it into actions of the log when the hand lets
go: a lit burner under a vessel is `heat` with the burner's power, a vessel standing in a bath is `bath` with its
temperature, a jar on the shelf of a pneumatic trough is `collector water`, a tube's ends are `stopper`, `connect` and
`collector`; a reagent bottle is a stock of the shelf — what is poured, dropped or spooned from it is an `add`.

`pour` and `filter` into it fail with `chem.cracked`. The consequences are part of the state, so a replay of the log gives
them again (`tests/bench.rs`, `mistakes_have_consequences_that_replay`).

Diagnostics of `read`: `equil.concentrated` and `equil.beyondActivityModel` (approx), `bench.beyondModel` (a reagent
outside the activity model was added), `thermal.unknownEnthalpy` (`species[]` whose enthalpy of formation is unknown:
their part of the heat is not counted), and `redox.unmodelled` (unmodelled; `oxidant`, `reductant` — ids of
`catalog.couples` — and `volts`): an oxidant and a reductant that are both present would react by more than 0.25 V
and no declared reaction covers the pair, so the bench says so rather than showing a mixture that quietly does
nothing. The oxidation of water and couples marked `inert` are left out.

What settling means: the aqueous network comes to equilibrium at the vessel's temperature — fine solids (`powder`,
`precipitate`) and the gases take part; in an open vessel dissolved gases leave as bubbles once their pressures add up
to the room's less the vapour pressure of water, in a closed one they share the head space; then the declared instant
reactions run; then the enthalpy of everything that changed (Σ n·ΔfH°, 298 K values) acts on the temperature through
the heat capacity of the water, the solids, the gas and the glass (dissolved ions count as nothing — the dilute
approximation of school calorimetry). Volumes are additive. Water boils by the IAPWS-IF97 saturation line with the
activity of water (Raoult); the surplus heat evaporates water.

Conventions of these four operations:

- Lengths are ångström, angles degrees, dipoles debye (physics convention: the vector points from the negative to the
  positive charge). Indices are zero-based. Geometry names are keys (`linear`, `bent`, `trigonalPlanar`,
  `trigonalPyramidal`, `tShaped`, `tetrahedral`, `seesaw`, `squarePlanar`, `trigonalBipyramidal`, `squarePyramidal`,
  `octahedral`, …; polyhedra `tetrahedron`, `octahedron`, `cube`, `cuboctahedron`, `anticuboctahedron`, …) — the
  interface holds the three-language names.
- Element properties. The module carries a table generated from sourced data (`mol/elements_data.rs`, written by
  `scripts/oracles/mol.py --elements` from mendeleev: atomic weight, Pauling electronegativity, covalent radius of
  Cordero et al., van der Waals radius, Shannon ionic radii, main oxidation states), so the viewer needs no data file
  to draw a molecule. A request may override any property per symbol with
  `elements: { "Na": { mass, en, rcov, rvdw, rion } }`; values passed this way take precedence. This is the one
  exception to “data are not compiled into the core”: these operations are stateless and have no `…SetData`.
- Lone pairs: a main-group atom with V valence electrons, formal charge q and bond orders summing to B keeps
  ⌊(V − q − B) / 2⌋ pairs (aromatic bonds count 1.5). `observed` is the geometry read from the coordinates; where it
  differs from the class the diagnostic `mol.geometryDiffers` (info) says so. d- and f-block centres get no class
  (`mol.vseprNotApplicable`, unmodelled).
- Dipole: Σ qᵢ rᵢ from the partial charges when the request has them; otherwise the sum of bond moments
  (1 − e^(−Δχ²/4))·e·d without lone-pair contributions, marked `mol.dipoleEstimated` (approx).
- Crystals: the first shell of a site is every neighbour within (1 + `tolerance`) of its shortest contact in the
  infinite lattice — its size is the coordination number. A first-shell contact is a bond when it is also at most
  `bondScale` times the sum of the covalent radii. Generated structure types: `sc`, `fcc`, `bcc`, `bct`, `hcp`,
  `dhcp`, `diamond`, `white-tin`, `graphite`, `rock-salt`, `caesium-chloride`, `zinc-blende`, `wurtzite`, `fluorite`,
  `rutile`, `perovskite`; any other type needs its `basis`.
- Orbitals: hydrogen-like ψ_nlm = R_nl·Y_lm with real spherical harmonics (m = 0 zonal, m > 0 the cos mφ and m < 0
  the sin |m|φ combination). The cloud is drawn from |ψ|² with xoshiro256** seeded through SplitMix64 from `seed`:
  the same request gives the same points on every build.

Notes on the reactor operations:

- **A teaching model** (D278): two-group diffusion on homogenised cells, point kinetics with six delayed groups,
  lumped thermal hydraulics, xenon and samarium. Every result carries the diagnostic `reactor.teachingModel` (info);
  the interface must show that the model is not for design or safety analysis. The cross-sections are teaching
  values, each with its source (`docs/chem/DATA_SOURCES.md`, "反应堆截面数据").
- **Boundary.** Thermal reactors in steady state and slow transients. Fuel is natural or low-enriched uranium (at
  most 5 %). When the reactivity reaches 0.9 β the integration stops: `state.ended = { reason: "validity" }` and the
  diagnostic `reactor.promptCritical` (unmodelled). Nothing is computed beyond; a later `reactorAdvance` returns the
  same state. The other end is `coreDamage` (`reactor.coreDamage`, error): the peak fuel temperature exceeded the
  damage temperature of the fuel.
- Solver (`reactor/diffusion.rs`): cell-centred finite volumes; each group's operator is factorised once by a banded
  Cholesky decomposition (a direct, fixed sequence of operations — no convergence criterion that could differ
  between platforms); k_eff by the Arnoldi method on the fission source, restarted with the Ritz vector until the
  relative residual is below `tol`. Measured: a 64 × 64 grid in 21 ms natively.
- Time (`reactor/reactor.rs`, `kinetics.rs`): ρ = ρ_static + Σ α·(x − x_base) + shim, integrated with the kinetics,
  the thermal model, the poisons and the decay heat by a linearly implicit Rosenbrock method (order 2, error
  estimate of order 3). The layout is solved again when the feedback has moved the reactivity by 0.2 β (plus a
  tenth of the reactivity far below critical) and at every tenth of rod travel; `solves` counts these solutions and
  `maxSolves` bounds them per call (`partial: true` and `reactor.solveBudget` when the time asked for was not
  reached — call again with the returned state).
- A control rod is a volume-weighted mixture of its cell with the rod material. In cells much larger than the
  diffusion length (20 cm cells in graphite or heavy water) most of the worth lies in the first third of the travel.
- `init`: without `power` the state is cold and shut down as the document describes it; with `power` it is the
  equilibrium at that power (temperatures, poisons, decay heat, precursors) and `shim` takes the reactivity that is
  left; `search: true` first finds the common rod insertion that makes the reactor critical. A positive `shim` is
  reported with `reactor.shimPositive` (approx): no absorber provides it.
- Other diagnostics: `reactor.trip` (info; `reason`: `power`, `period`, `fuelTemperature`, `manual`),
  `reactor.notConverged` (approx).
